Laboratory evaluation of FINER completed

The integrated test of the full FINER system at NAOJ, including the warm optics, both front-end receivers, the local oscillators, the IF chain and the DRS4 spectrometers, has been completed. FINER is now being prepared for its journey to the LMT in Mexico.

The FINER "periscope" is ready

The warm optics of FINER, a coupling optical mechanism that picks up the radio waves collected by the LMT and leads them to the FINER receiver, is ready. Tatsuya Takekoshi at Kitami Institute of Technology is finalizing its integration.

FINER receiver used for a dark photon search (DOSUE-RR)

Before its shipment to the LMT, the FINER Band 6+7 receiver (210–350 GHz) is being used at NAOJ by DOSUE-RR, an experiment that searches for dark matter with radio waves. The team led by Okayama University, including Associate Professor Adachi, installed its own calibration and readout system on FINER and started the search for dark photons in the 0.9–1.4 meV mass range on 6 April 2026. See the announcement by Okayama University (in Japanese).

About FINER

FINER (Far-Infrared Nebular Emission Receiver) is a dual-polarization sideband-separating superconductor-insulator-superconductor (SIS) mixer receiver for the Large Millimeter Telescope (LMT) in Mexico. By exploiting ALMA’s new wideband sensitivity upgrade (WSU) technology, FINER covers a wide range in radio frequencies from 120–350 GHz, with an instantaneous intermediate frequency (IF) of 4–20 GHz per sideband per polarization, providing 40% of ALMAʼs light-collecting area, a similar atmospheric transmittance to ALMA, and a 5 times wider bandwidth than the current ALMA, which offers the most sensitive spectral-scanning capability among millimeter and submillimeter telescopes in the northern hemisphere.

The laboratory integration and evaluation of the full FINER system were completed at the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan (NAOJ) in October 2026, and the system is being prepared for installation on the LMT. FINER is expected to come online in 2027.

The FINER project is supported by JSPS KAKENHI: Grant-in-Aid for Scientific Research (S) 22H04939 (PI: Y. Tamura), Specially Promoted Research 24H00004 and International Leading Research 23K20035 (PI: K. Kohno), and Grant-in-Aid for Scientific Research (A) 15H02073 (PI: R. Kawabe).
