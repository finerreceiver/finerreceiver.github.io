System overview

At first light FINER has two front-end receivers in a single cryostat: a Band 4 MMIC receiver and a wideband Band 6+7 receiver, both developed at NAOJ. A wideband Band 4+5 receiver covering 120–210 GHz is planned as a later upgrade. IF switches select which band feeds the four IF chains, so one band is observed at a time; simultaneous dual-band observation is an option for a future optics upgrade.

Optics

The FINER warm optics picks off the beam after the LMT’s M4 mirror with a pair of motorized mirrors (the “periscope”), which can be set and stowed remotely. A chopper with a blackbody load is used for intensity calibration. A fast sky chopper for quick position switching is planned in a later phase. The spatial resolution (PSF size) is about 7 arcsec × (frequency / 200 GHz)−1.

Front-end receivers

The front-end receivers exploit the high critical current density SIS mixer technology developed for the ALMA Wideband Sensitivity Upgrade (WSU) (Kojima et al. 2017, 2020; Kang et al. 2026), which provides a wide instantaneous frequency coverage.

Back-end spectrometers

  • Wideband spectrometers (DRS4, ELECS Inc.). Two DRS4 chassis digitize eight 10.24 GHz-wide basebands with 3-bit, 20.48 GSa/s analog-to-digital converters and provide spectra with 20 MHz resolution (Hagimoto et al. 2024). Sideband separation is also performed digitally.
  • High-resolution spectrometers (XFFTS, RPG). The XFFTS spectrometers of the LMT 2-mm receiver B4R (2.5 GHz bandwidth, 88 kHz resolution) remain available for high-resolution spectroscopy.

Data and software

FINER is controlled from the LMT Telescope Control System. The data format and the reduction pipeline are being developed together with the LMT team.
