## Galaxy formation in the first half of the epoch of reionization

### Background

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has found more than a hundred candidate galaxies at redshifts *z* > 8, when the Universe was less than about 600 million years old, and has confirmed some of them spectroscopically out to *z* ≈ 14. The bright end of their ultraviolet luminosity function evolves surprisingly little with redshift ([Harikane et al. 2022](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJS..259...20H/abstract)), which means that massive galaxies had to grow very efficiently within a short time. This places a stringent constraint on models of cosmic structure formation.

To understand what drives this rapid growth, we need spectroscopy that answers two questions:

- How common are bright, massive galaxies in the early epoch of reionization (their number density)?
- What physical conditions control their growth (metallicity, ionization state and gas density)?

### Far-infrared nebular emission lines

The far-infrared fine-structure line of doubly ionized oxygen, [O III] 88 µm, was proposed as a powerful tracer for identifying the redshifts of the most distant galaxies ([Inoue et al. 2014](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...780L..18I/abstract)). ALMA observations of this line revealed extremely high [O III]/[C II] ratios, indicating highly ionized gas filling these young galaxies ([Inoue et al. 2016](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016Sci...352.1559I/abstract)), and held the record for the most distant spectroscopic redshift obtained with ALMA until 2022 ([Hashimoto et al. 2018](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018Natur.557..392H/abstract)). Even in the JWST era, [O III] 88 µm at *z* > 9 is as detectable as the rest-frame ultraviolet lines observed with JWST/NIRSpec.

However, ALMA in the southern hemisphere cannot easily observe high-declination fields. Many bright galaxies at *z* > 8 found in northern deep fields, such as GOODS-North and CEERS, remain unexplored in the far-infrared lines (Figure 1).

### Key science goals

With FINER on the 50-m LMT in Mexico (latitude 19°N), we aim to:

- identify the spectroscopic redshifts of bright galaxies at *z* ≈ 8–15 in the northern sky using [O III] 88 µm and [C II] 158 µm, including galaxies confirmed by JWST and photometric candidates from wide-field surveys such as *Euclid* and *Roman*;
- detect [O III] 88 µm from a bright Lyman break galaxy in a single night of observation;
- measure the number density of bright galaxies in the first half of the epoch of reionization and diagnose their interstellar medium.

### How FINER helps

A typical photometric redshift uncertainty of Δ*z* ≈ 1 at *z* ≈ 10 requires a spectral coverage of more than 30 GHz to find the line. FINER covers about 38 GHz in a single tuning, whereas the current ALMA needs four or more tunings for the same range (Figure 2). Combined with 40% of ALMA's collecting area and a high-altitude site (4,600 m), this offers the most sensitive spectral-scanning capability among millimeter and submillimeter telescopes in the northern hemisphere (Figure 3).

## Further science cases

FINER also offers unique capabilities for a broad range of science, including:

- the Solar System (planetary atmospheres and comets),
- the interstellar medium and astrochemistry,
- star formation,
- nearby galaxies,
- distant dusty star-forming galaxies, including spectroscopic follow-up of galaxies detected with TolTEC.
